Understanding Your Well's Safety Margin

Think of your well like a straw in a glass of water. The safety margin (or "buffer") is how much water is above the bottom of your straw (pump). If the water level drops below your straw, you'll suck air instead of water.

Warning Signs to Watch For

Contact a well professional if you notice any of these signs:

  • Reduced water pressure in your taps or shower
  • Pump running more frequently or for longer periods
  • Air spitting from faucets when you first turn them on
  • Pump making unusual noises or cycling on and off rapidly
  • Cloudy or muddy water (could indicate low water levels)
  • Complete loss of water - turn off your pump immediately

Water Conservation Tips

Whether your well is high-risk or low-risk, these conservation measures can help:

High-Impact Actions:

  • Fix leaks immediately - A dripping tap can waste thousands of liters per year
  • Install low-flow fixtures - Showerheads, toilets, and faucet aerators
  • Run full loads - Only run dishwasher and washing machine when full
  • Take shorter showers - Even 2 minutes less makes a big difference

During Dry Periods:

  • Space out water usage - Don't run multiple appliances at once
  • Collect rainwater for gardens and outdoor use
  • Limit lawn watering - Grass will recover, wells may not
  • Use greywater where safe (dishwater for plants, etc.)

Professional Help

When to call a well professional:

  • Your well is rated as High Risk or Critical
  • You notice any warning signs listed above
  • Your water usage needs have increased significantly
  • It's been more than 10 years since a professional inspection

What they can do:

  • Water level measurement - Accurate current assessment
  • Pump inspection and adjustment - Lower pump if possible
  • Well rehabilitation - Cleaning and restoration techniques
  • Well deepening - If geology allows
  • Water quality testing - Ensure safety
  • System efficiency improvements - Better pumps, pressure tanks

Finding a professional: Look for certified well drillers or pump installers in Nova Scotia. Check with the Nova Scotia Ground Water Association or your local health authority for recommendations.
